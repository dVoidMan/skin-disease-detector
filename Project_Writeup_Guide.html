CHAPTER ONE: INTRODUCTION

1.1 Background of the Study

Dermatological disorders are among the most widespread health conditions globally, affecting individuals across all age groups and socioeconomic backgrounds. Early and accurate identification of skin lesions is critical to preventing chronic progression, disfigurement, and secondary infections. However, in many developing nations and underserved communities, certified dermatologists are scarce and concentrated primarily in major urban centers. Consequently, patients often face prolonged wait times, high consultation fees, or rely on inaccurate self-medication.

Recent breakthroughs in Artificial Intelligence (AI), Computer Vision, and Deep Learning—most notably Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs)—have demonstrated diagnostic capabilities comparable to clinical specialists in visual pattern recognition tasks. By training deep neural networks on curated datasets of dermatological images, an automated screening system can analyze subtle morphological features such as lesion borders, color variations, scaling, and texture to provide rapid preliminary assessments via a web browser.

1.2 Abstract

Skin diseases represent one of the most prevalent global health challenges, affecting millions of individuals across diverse demographics. Early and accurate identification is critical to preventing severe complications; however, access to certified dermatologists remains limited, particularly in underserved and rural regions, leading to delayed diagnosis and self-medication. This project presents the design and implementation of an AI-Based Skin Disease Detection System that leverages Deep Learning—specifically Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) and Transfer Learning—to classify common dermatological conditions from digital skin images. Images uploaded by users undergo automated preprocessing using OpenCV and Pillow (resizing, RGB normalization, and noise reduction) before being analyzed by a trained TensorFlow/Keras model capable of identifying conditions such as Acne, Eczema, Psoriasis, Ringworm (Tinea), Vitiligo, and Dermatitis. The model is integrated into a responsive web application built with the Python Flask framework, HTML5, CSS3, and JavaScript, backed by a relational database (SQLite/MySQL) for managing user accounts and historical diagnostic logs. In addition to displaying the predicted condition and probability confidence scores, the platform provides curated educational overviews and precautionary guidance while encouraging timely clinical consultation.

1.3 Statement of the Problem

  1. Scarcity of Specialist Dermatologists: The low ratio of dermatologists to patients in many regions leads to severe bottlenecks in primary healthcare delivery.
  2. High Cost of Preliminary Screening: Routine physical consultations for minor or emerging rashes can be financially prohibitive for many individuals.
  3. Visual Overlap Among Skin Disorders: Conditions such as Eczema, Psoriasis, and Ringworm share overlapping visual traits (redness, scaling, itching), resulting in frequent misdiagnosis by non-specialists.
  4. Delayed Medical Intervention: Lack of accessible awareness tools leads patients to ignore early symptoms until conditions become severe or contagious.

1.4 Aim and Objectives of the Study

Aim: To design and implement an AI-based web application for the early detection and classification of common skin diseases using Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN).

Specific Objectives:

  1. To acquire and preprocess a clinical dataset of skin disease images using OpenCV and Pillow (resizing, color conversion, normalization, and augmentation).
  2. To build and train a deep learning classification model using TensorFlow and Keras based on Transfer Learning.
  3. To evaluate the trained model using standard performance metrics including Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1-Score, and a Confusion Matrix.
  4. To develop an interactive web interface using Flask, HTML5, CSS3, and JavaScript that enables users to upload skin photographs and receive real-time diagnostic predictions and confidence scores.
  5. To design a relational database (SQLite/MySQL) for storing user credentials, scan history, and educational disease information.

1.5 Scope and Limitations

  • Scope: The system classifies macroscopic images across seven classes (Acne, Eczema, Psoriasis, Ringworm, Vitiligo, Contact Dermatitis, and Healthy Skin), provides confidence percentages, logs user scan histories, and presents curated medical precautions.
  • Limitations: The system is a preliminary decision-support tool and does not replace histological biopsy or physical examination by a licensed physician. Image quality, poor lighting, and camera blur can affect prediction confidence.

CHAPTER TWO: LITERATURE REVIEW & THEORETICAL CONCEPTS

2.1 Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) in Medical Imaging

A Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) is a specialized deep learning architecture designed to process grid-structured data such as digital images. Unlike traditional machine learning algorithms that require hand-engineered feature extraction, CNNs automatically learn spatial hierarchies of features through backpropagation:

  • Convolutional Layers: Apply learnable filters across the input image to detect edges, pigment boundaries, and lesion textures.
  • Pooling Layers: Down-sample feature maps to reduce computational complexity and achieve spatial invariance.
  • Fully Connected & Softmax Layers: Map extracted high-level representations into class probability scores across target skin diseases.

2.2 Mathematical Formulations

1. 2D Convolution Operation: For an input image $I$ and kernel $K$ of size $M \times N$:

$$S(i, j) = (I * K)(i, j) = \sum_{m=0}^{M-1} \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} I(i + m, j + n) \cdot K(m, n) + b$$

2. ReLU Activation Function: Introduces non-linearity by zeroing negative values:

$$f(x) = \max(0, x)$$

3. Softmax Probability Function: Converts raw output logits $z_i$ for $C$ classes into normalized probabilities:

$$P(y = i \mid x) = \frac{e^{z_i}}{\sum_{j=1}^{C} e^{z_j}}$$

4. Categorical Cross-Entropy Loss: Measures divergence between ground-truth labels $y_i$ and predicted probabilities $\hat{y}_i$:

$$\mathcal{L}(y, \hat{y}) = -\sum_{i=1}^{C} y_i \log(\hat{y}_i)$$

Class ID Skin Condition Visual / Clinical Presentation Primary Symptoms & Advice
0 Acne Vulgaris Comedones (blackheads/whiteheads), erythematous papules, pustules, or nodules on sebaceous areas. Local inflammation, oily skin; avoid squeezing lesions and use non-comedogenic cleansers.

4 Vitiligo Well-circumscribed chalky-white depigmented macules and patches due to melanocyte loss. Painless depigmentation; protect depigmented skin with broad-spectrum sunscreen.
5 Contact Dermatitis Localized redness, swelling, and small vesicles/blisters strictly at sites of allergen/irritant exposure. Burning, stinging, itching; identify and discontinue contact with the causative substance.
6 Healthy / Normal Skin Uniform skin tone, intact epidermal surface, absence of erythema, scaling, or lesions. Control class used to prevent false-positive diagnoses on unaffected skin.

CHAPTER FOUR: SYSTEM IMPLEMENTATION AND TESTING

4.1 Justification of Selected Technologies

  • Python: Selected as the core language due to its rich ecosystem for deep learning, image processing, and web development.
  • TensorFlow & Keras: Used to build, train, and serialize the Convolutional Neural Network (.keras / .h5) with GPU acceleration support.
  • OpenCV & Pillow: Employed for image decoding, RGB channel standardization, and spatial resizing ($224 \times 224$).
  • Flask: A lightweight Python WSGI micro-framework ideal for hosting deep learning models and serving HTML templates with minimal overhead.
  • HTML5, CSS3 & JavaScript: Provide a responsive medical interface, interactive drag-and-drop file upload, client-side image preview (via FileReader API), and dynamic confidence progress bars.
  • SQLite / MySQL: Relational database management system integrated via SQLAlchemy ORM to persist user accounts and diagnostic logs.
  • Google Colab / Jupyter Notebook & VS Code: Google Colab provides cloud GPU resources for model training, while VS Code is used for full-stack application development.

4.2 Evaluation Metrics for the Deep Learning Model

$$\text{Accuracy} = \frac{TP + TN}{TP + TN + FP + FN} \qquad \text{Precision} = \frac{TP}{TP + FP}$$ $$\text{Recall (Sensitivity)} = \frac{TP}{TP + FN} \qquad \text{F1-Score} = 2 \times \frac{\text{Precision} \times \text{Recall}}{\text{Precision} + \text{Recall}}$$

CHAPTER FIVE: CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS

5.1 Conclusion

The AI-Based Skin Disease Detection System successfully demonstrates the practical application of Convolutional Neural Networks and Transfer Learning in preliminary healthcare screening. By bridging a trained TensorFlow/Keras model with an accessible Flask web interface and relational database, the system empowers users to obtain rapid, objective preliminary assessments of common dermatological conditions while promoting early consultation with certified medical professionals.

5.2 Recommendations for Future Work

  1. Explainable AI (Grad-CAM Visualization): Incorporating Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping to highlight the specific visual regions of the skin lesion that influenced the CNN's prediction.
  2. Offline Mobile Application: Exporting the trained model to TensorFlow Lite (.tflite) for native offline execution on Android and iOS smartphones in remote areas without internet connectivity.
  3. Multimodal Patient Metadata: Combining visual image classification with structured clinical symptoms (e.g., duration of rash, presence of fever, itch intensity, patient age) to further improve diagnostic precision.
  4. Tele-Dermatology Integration: Enabling direct sharing of scan reports with verified dermatologists for remote clinical follow-up.
